### How to paint this subject:

**Step 0: Identify the hue on the metal**
Is the metal casing warm or cool? Or neutral?
**Cool** = black paint + blue (Neutral Tint, Payne's Grey, Lamp Black etc)
**Warm** = black paint + warm (burnt sienna or orange)
**Neutral** = blue + burnt sienna or "Jane's Grey" from Daniel Smith. This allows the flexibility to be toned cooler or warmer from the base color by adding either more blue or sienna to the mix.
**Light areas/highlights**: Use Warm Grey (Rosa), Ultramarine Ash or Mineral Grey from Winsor & Newton if not left completely bare

**Step 1: ID contrasts**
Start by identifying light source and contrasts. If needed (optional), map the contrast on the reference photo or printed photo of the subject - otherwise use phone camera settings or photo editing in black and white to identify shadows. Use a medium sized brush, e.g. brush size 6 or 4, depending on the subject's size.

**Step 2: Base layer**
Begin by adding a base layer of the main color, using wet-in-wet to let granulating colors flow etc. When done and dry, add a second base layer, this time, making sure to avoid the highlighted areas. Use a smaller brush for this, e.g. size 4 or similar.

**Step 4: Contrast layers**
Add more paint/darker mixes to the dark areas on the metal by adding a thicker mix of darker paint. Use more black/blue in the mix.

**Step 5: Details/highlights**
With a small brush, add shadows to the small parts, or below the small parts, e.g. on metal screws, hinges on the front wheel, train lights, bells etc. Use a mix of either base layer or another dark color for shadows. If the highlighted areas has been painted over, use either a moist brush to lift paint, or white paint to restore them.
