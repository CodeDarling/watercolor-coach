### How to paint this subject:

It can be adviseable to use masking fluid to paint this flower, as the flower stems risk being easy to paint on/over. Often the stamens need to be a light hue/value, where only the tips on the filaments needs a darker black or blue color. Thus, remember to use masking fluid where applicable.

**Step 1: ID shadows and highlights**
As this flower has many flower bulbs/head on a single flower stem, identifying shadows and highlights add to the realism and make painting easier. With Neutral Tint or botanical grey, add the required shadows in the crevices and shadow areas. If you have a reference photo, use your phone to add a black-and-white filter - or, if not, decide the direction of the light source and take in reflected shadows, highlights as well as the core of shadows (James Gurney)

**Step 2: First wash**
Brush size: Small (e.g. 3, 2 or less)
Using a mix of blues, add the first washes to the flower with a very light wash - e.g. Cobalt Blue, Ultramarine or a mix of blue with a lilac/pink, depending on the base color on the flower (e.g. purple or blueish. Ensure that highlights are preserved!
Work each flower or flower petal individually.
Repeat this proces after the first layer is dry, to add depth to the first layers of colors. Vary between blue and/or purple washes.
The middle of the petals are often darker, so make sure to see which color these have

**Step 3: Shadows and details**
Using a size 1 or 0 brush, add the darker areas on the flower with e.g. Blue-Violet PV15 or Smalt Blue as a difference color, as this is often an accent color on the petal. Add dark areas in the tips of each petal and/or along the center ridge on each petal. Build up color gradually with thin layers to add depth. Take care to preserve the highlighted areas, as the contrast adds to this special flower's special appeal.

**Step 4: Flower stamens and filaments**
If masking fluid is used, remove the gum, and with a size 000 brush, paint the stamens with a very thin brush, e.g. size 000 or smaller, depending on the size of the subject. Sometimes the stamens are white, and sometimes light blue - make a thin wash of e.g. PV15 or a bluish purple and use black/neutral tint to paint the small filamnts. 

**Step 5: Each flower's green stem**
With a light green (e.g. Sap Green), paint each thin stem from each flower with a size 000 brush and add shadows where the stems connect to the flower bulb OR the main flower stem below, to add depth.
