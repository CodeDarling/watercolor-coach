### How to paint this subject: Flower petals

The petals are painted first. Then the centre of the flowers with the black and blue stamens + loaded pollen tips (filaments) are painted on top, using wet-on-dry and a drawing technique. In other words, it does not matter, if you paint over these small stamens during the process of painting the petals, as these will be over-painted afterwards with black.

**Step 0: Identify light and dark areas**
Using either a botanical grey or neutral tint, lightly add the shadowy parts of the petals with a small brush for a more controlled result. Leave and let dry.

**Step 1: First wash**
Mix a wash with blue and a touch of lilac/rose and lay down a very gentle wash on the whole petal. Repeat the proces on the rest of the petals. Notice where the petals are more violet than blue, as the flower has several hues between light purple and all the way to the darkest of blues, almost black in the crevices on the petals. Adjust the mixes accordingly. 
Use a medium sized brush for this, e.g. size 4 or 5.

**Step 2: Add more layers to the flowers**
Add a single color to the layering process in between glazing/layering with mixes. This build up glow and transparency on the petals. 
Brush size: 3, 2 or 1

**Step 3: Add veins or darker crevices on the petals**
By adding a darker mix strategically using the wet-in-damp technique, at the same time as Step 2 is done, can build up depth of color and natural shadows on the petals. Make sure to keep the highlighted areas light! Use the blue/pink/lilac mix - or use the Smalt Blue from Winsor & Newton for the perfect touch of "glow".
Add extra layers of paint onto the shadows, to give depth of contrast on veins or folds on the petals. 
This require a small brush, e.g. 1, 0 or 00, depending on the size of the flower petal. 

*STEP 3 IS ESSENTIAL THE BUILDING STAGE AND CAN TAKE A LONG TIME, AS LAYERS ARE BUILT GRADUALLY FROM SCRATCH.*

**Step 4: Add detailed veins**
With a size 00 or 000 brush, add thin, dark veins onto the petals to indicate the veins of the petal. Immediately after, soften the edges with a moist, soft brush tip, to make the petals look natural and a part of the deeper layers in the petals.

**Step 5: Drybrush petals**
With a dry, loaded brush size 2 or 3, brush gently on top of the darkest parts of the petals, to give the overall velvety softness of the flower.


### How to paint this subject: centre of open blooms

**Step 1: Drawing with the brush**
Use a very thin brush, size 000, with a good snap and spring, to literally draw the stamens and filaments (the tip of the stamens). Make sure to be very precise and remove any excess paint to increase precision of lines.

**Step 2: Paint the centre piece**
Using a size 00 brush, add a light layer of Hansa Yellow Medium to the round button in the middle. Repeat with a wash of Green Gold. It's perfect if the yellow and gren mute some of the filaments sharpness. 

**Step 3: Add hairs and more stamens**
With the tip of the 000 brush, add very very small hairs on the round button with a black/dark mix. After this, add more stamens and filaments around the "hairy button" slowly and methodically. Make it look random, to indicate a sense of realism.
